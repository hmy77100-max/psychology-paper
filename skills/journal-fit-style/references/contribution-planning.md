# Author-led contribution planning before the outline

Trigger after journal selection when the author requests journal-guided outline or whole-manuscript revision. Do not equate journal fit with a contribution or start rearranging the manuscript before the author chooses the working direction.

Read the current manuscript and preserve its original argument, design, actual findings and evidence ceiling. Examine the journal's relevant research trajectory and recent related work using verifiable primary sources, alongside direct predecessors from the wider field. Separate journal style from scientific novelty. Journal acceptance and publication are not promised.

Deliver recommendations, not a replacement decision:

- Reconstruct the author's present question and reasoning, including valuable parts that should remain.
- Identify what research value is still extractable: the unresolved problem, closest predecessors, what this study actually adds and whom that addition helps. Attach sources to comparison claims and disclose incomplete searching.
- Offer only defensible contribution angles. For each, explain evidence, incremental value, counterevidence/limits, fit with journal readers, and the implications for the introduction–methods–results–discussion chain. A localized scale, sample or setting alone is not automatically a theoretical innovation.
- Distinguish supported findings, plausible interpretation and untested novelty. If the available study cannot support a strong angle, state that limitation rather than manufacturing one.
- Ask for the author's own view: they can accept, combine, reject or describe a better question in unrestricted language. Do not force a numbered option or fixed approval command.

Useful author input is their intended question/contribution, reasons, findings to foreground and non-negotiable boundaries—not internal plugin fields. Ask only for what their response leaves materially unclear. Once the author confirms a working direction, record their actual response, contribution statement and outline implications. Only then develop the outline. A confirmed working direction is not proof of novelty and does not pre-approve later prose.
