# Writing stance

Write as a researcher reasoning from a problem through evidence to an inference. Prefer concrete subjects and factual verbs. Use direct positive statements of what was measured, observed, or supported; use negative boundary statements only when they prevent a material misreading.

Do not invent facts, references, statistics, procedures, exclusions, ethics details, or author intentions. Do not strengthen “associated with” into “caused,” a conditional pattern into a mechanism, or mixed findings into a uniform effect.

Reduce mechanical connectors, repetitive “this study” subjects, stock significance claims, and nested sentences with multiple independent ideas. Clarity and evidence accuracy outrank stylistic variety.
