# Theory reasoning for the author's current question

Use for theoretical bridges, competing predictions or what a result adds, as the task requires. Ordinary wording edits do not need this resource. Reuse it while applicable; examples are separately optional. These are reasoning options, not stages, a compulsory checklist or a manuscript outline.

## Locate the assumption that matters

Separate a theory's core proposition from the bridging or auxiliary assumptions used in this study. For example, a proposition about processing styles does not itself establish that a particular message induces those styles. Identify what the proposed inference depends on: the construct, operation, measurement, implementation or generalization. Explain which link a result informs and which it leaves open. Do not remove a useful theoretical source merely because a process was not measured, or treat an operation as proof of that process. Preserve the original hypothesis and its actual planning history.

## Compare accounts on the same phenomenon

Start from the finding or question that needs explanation. Ask what each relevant account predicts under the actual conditions and what assumption produces that prediction. Use the strongest relevant version of a published account, not a convenient caricature. Separate source claims from the assistant's proposed explanation; give inspectable support for attributed claims using the current source-evidence guidance.

Compare concrete differences such as operations, participants, outcomes and incentives. If two studies differ on several dimensions, those differences suggest explanations; they do not identify which difference caused the result. Identify the most useful distinction rather than listing every possible mechanism. A compact comparison can help, but no table, number of rivals or new study is compulsory.

## Identify the evidential role

A result can support a behavioral prediction while remaining compatible with several mechanisms. State what is supported and how far a process interpretation travels. A nonsignificant contrast is not equivalence, and support in one condition plus nonsupport in another is not itself an interaction. Distinguish an observed boundary from a proposed failure condition and from insufficient information.

For an actual formal model, distinguish mathematical derivation, accommodation of known results through fitting, and predictions assessed without tuning to those outcomes. State the relevant fitting or selection history; good fit alone does not establish a uniquely correct mechanism. Do not retrofit an inspected result into a prior prediction. When no formal model is involved, do not introduce parameter checks, simulations or model selection just to use this resource.

## Make the reasoning useful to the author

Explain what the present evidence adds relative to the relevant predecessor: a discriminating result, an application, a boundary, a measurement advance or a consequential extension can each matter. Do not require every paper to defeat a theory or make a novel formal prediction. Show why a proposed interpretation or wording follows from the available evidence, and offer a concrete candidate when requested. Keep the author's viable route; another well-supported route may be preferable for their question and journal.

For source-based claims use the current source-evidence guidance: original short support, locator and access tier. Keep useful abstract-supported recommendations affirmative and labeled "摘要级别支持；全文未取得" (or its translation). The author decides adoption or further checking. Do not invent unreported details or turn access notes into manuscript caveats.

Keep the explanation to the author distinct from the requested manuscript candidate. If a candidate labels a mechanism as a possible explanation, appending another generic "not measured/cannot establish" sentence may repeat the same qualification and displace the substantive judgment. Check whether that sentence adds a necessary boundary in context; preserve it when it does. Otherwise develop what the evidence helps explain or decide. This is a redundancy diagnosis, not a caveat quota or required ending. Neither a quotation nor author approval independently proves an inference.
