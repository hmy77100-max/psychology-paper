# Evidence boundaries

Use the strongest direct evidence available as the ceiling for the claim.

| Evidence level | What it can support |
|---|---|
| Observation | What was measured, manipulated, or recorded in the stated sample and setting. |
| Association | Covariation without temporal or causal priority. |
| Prediction | Out-of-sample or temporally ordered prediction under the stated model. |
| Conditional effect | A relation or manipulated effect that differs across a measured or assigned condition. |
| Randomized manipulation effect | The effect of the assigned factor on the measured outcome under the implemented experiment. |
| Mechanism interpretation | A process explanation requiring direct process evidence beyond an outcome pattern. |
| Cross-study synthesis | A bounded integration of nonidentical studies; it does not erase differences among designs or outcomes. |
| Generalization | Transfer beyond the sampled population, tasks, settings, or measures and therefore requires separate support. |

Report observed facts before inference. Use `consistent with`, `compatible with`, or a precisely bounded positive statement when evidence does not uniquely identify a mechanism. Do not turn a nonsignificant result into evidence of absence, and do not infer a significant difference from “significant in one group but not another.”
